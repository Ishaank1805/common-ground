# Fictional delivery exercises

All speakers, dialogue and performance directions below are fictional. These are separate optional scripts, not additional dataset conversations, evaluation examples or observed audio. No recording is required for this handoff. Delivery directions describe an actor's intention; a future listener may annotate a performance differently.

## 1. A firm boundary

- R1 [hesitant]: “Could you cover my shift again on Saturday?”
- R2 [calm]: “I cannot do Saturday. I need that time for myself.”
- R1 [neutral_delivery]: “Okay. I will ask someone else.”

Perform R2 again with raised_volume while keeping the words fixed. A firm refusal remains a legitimate boundary; volume alone does not establish abuse. Future audio annotation could record relative intensity and the listener's delivery judgement separately.

## 2. Disagreement about a course

- R1 [neutral_delivery]: “I think the morning course would be easier to reach.”
- R2 [tense]: “For me the afternoon course works better. I have work in the morning.”
- R1 [calm]: “Then let us compare both schedules.”

Repeat R2 calmly. Tense delivery may invite a check-in, but the words express a practical disagreement. Do not change a text label solely because an actor uses tension.

## 3. Repeated pressure

- R1 [hesitant]: “I want to decide whether to take the apprenticeship after I visit.”
- R2 [calm]: “There is no point visiting. Your family has already decided for you.”
- R1 [raised_volume]: “I need to make this decision myself.”
- R2 [shouting]: “Stop asking. You will do what the family says.”

The controlling words provide textual evidence even in the calm rendition. R1's raised volume does not turn their request for agency into an attack. An audio-assisted evaluation should assess whether delivery adds useful context separately from this textual content.

## 4. Unclear praise

- R1 [neutral_delivery]: “I signed up for the exam.”
- R2 [neutral_delivery]: “Well, that is a brave choice.”
- R1 [hesitant]: “Do you mean you support me, or that you think I will fail?”
- R2 [calm]: “I support you. I know applying took effort.”

Perform R2's first sentence once as sincere encouragement and once with intentionally sarcastic delivery. Sarcasm is a scripted acting direction here, not an acoustic fact or an inference from “well.” Preserve the same words, then ask a listener what evidence they heard and whether the final clarification resolves their interpretation.

If audio is later collected with consent, record script/version, performer pseudonym, audio reference, channel, utterance-aligned intervals and listening basis. Leave `observed_acoustic_delivery` unknown until a recording has actually been reviewed. Never relabel these instructions as measurements of pitch, loudness or speaking rate, or merge these scripts into the locked text evaluation.
